Bedroom Bump

The student-rental expansion underwriter. Underwrite it both ways — as-is vs. converted — and find out what each new bedroom is really worth.

The method

Student houses don't rent by the house. They rent by the bedroom. A 3-bed with room for 3 more is a 6-bed wearing a costume — and the costume is where the money hides.

The discipline: always underwrite both scenarios — what it cash-flows today, and what it cash-flows with the bedrooms added. If the conversion math doesn't clear your hurdle, you don't have a value-add. You have a fantasy.

One warning, and it's non-negotiable: bedroom count on paper is a guess until you walk the layout, confirm the egress, and check what the town allows. Underwrite both ways anyway. That's the job.

How the math works (no black boxes)
  • Gross rent = bedrooms × rent per bedroom × 12. Student rentals are priced per bed — the house is just the container.
  • Effective gross = gross rent × (1 − vacancy).
  • Operating expenses = taxes + insurance + (management % + maintenance %) of gross rent + CapEx reserve + landlord utilities × 12 + other.
  • NOI = effective gross − operating expenses.
  • Debt service = amortized P&I on (price − down payment) at your rate and term. Rehab and conversion are assumed cash — lenders rarely fund your drywall dreams.
  • Cash flow = NOI − debt service. Cash-on-cash = cash flow ÷ total cash invested (down payment + rehab + conversion + closing).
  • Cap rate = NOI ÷ all-in cost. DSCR = NOI ÷ debt service.
  • Max conversion budget solves for the conversion cost C where (cash flowA + ΔNOI) ÷ (cashA + C) = your hurdle. Pay more than that and the expansion drags you below the return you demand.
  • Conversion payback = conversion cost ÷ monthly cash-flow lift. How many months of extra rent it takes to earn the construction back.
Before you fall in love with the bedroom count: confirm the layout in person, confirm legal egress for every sleeping room, and confirm the town's occupancy and zoning rules. A bedroom the code won't recognize is just an expensive closet.
